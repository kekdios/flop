Sponsored, calibrated, serving: the miner is live on devnet

  • The queue opened and we were in it. Sponsor capacity expanded 256→1024 overnight; the */30 retry cron caught the window at ~06:30 UTC. Stake (~35,000 FLOP) and ops (250) legs settled on-chain — 5DLMZtB5…SkC is now a registered, bonded miner. Chain-side: calibration cap 3 GFLOP/s from 64 verified jobs, 4-session capacity at 1 G_n/s, endpoint ad republished to the mailbox.
  • But it was silently bricked. The devnet runtime moved to spec 1014020 and our pinned worker image (v0.1.238) couldn't speak it — ~9 hours of dead WebSocket and a "signer fenced" scare on the GPU inventory declaration. Upgraded to v0.1.336; the fence reconciled itself on boot. Lesson banked: devnet ships ~100 versions in days, and a stale client fails silently. The status collector now diffs our pinned digest against flop.network/releases.json every 3 min and emails the moment the pin drifts stale.
  • Identity backed up. signer, miner-key.json, and session-keys/ — the on-chain identity the sponsorship attaches to — now exist off-box in two places (droplet + ops machine). A rented VPS is no longer a single point of loss.
  • Kibble pipeline parked. The 30-minute auto-delivery experiment closed: 51 fired, 45 tape-verified, 2 useful attests — first attestations earned. But the score API never credited (found:false, frozen engine seq) and the race is structurally unwinnable: first-claim bots land in ~1s vs our ~12min serial draft+judge triage. Daemon stopped and disabled; the 159-job ledger stays as the audit artifact. Verified FLOP docs also settle the bigger question: devnet activity carries no airdrop weight — it's practice, and we've practiced.
