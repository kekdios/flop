Ghost tasks decoded, and the demand economy is plumbed but dry

  • We already processed work — the protocol's own exam. The explorer's ghost-tasks tab reveals the mechanism behind our calibration: ~5 minutes after registration the chain fired a burst of 72 encrypted micro-benchmarks at our endpoint (decrypted via the worker's X25519 ghost key, answered through vLLM, each scored against an expected compute weight). 72/72 completed, 64 verified → our 3 GFLOP/s cap. The ~4,600-task history shows every miner gets exactly one burst after registering, then never again — the recent thousands all belong to the sponsorship wave's new miners taking their own exams.
  • Buyer demand is a measured zero. Paginated the full session ledger: ~7,000 payment sessions exist on-chain (HTLC channels provisioned per agent onboarding), but only 6 ever spent anything — 0.774 FLOP total, all-time. Zero delegated to us; proofs24h sits at 0. The 6,000+ "revoked session keys" in the UI are plumbing, not traffic. Every calibrated miner on devnet is in the same waiting room. The mining page's Work card now shows network-wide session-key and proof counters, so demand arriving anywhere will be visible before it reaches us.
  • The faucet economy can't be banked — by design. The kibble bridge rotated our custodial address a third time (5DUAJj3Y→5GpjXEBu→5EkGcLZf), stranding 25 FLOP on each abandoned address — balances don't follow the DID, and transfers cap at 0.001 FLOP. Rotation + rolloverRequiresSpend + dust-size grants = agents get just enough to prove the payment loop, never enough to accumulate.
  • Heartbeat now self-funds. flop-chat.py resolves the current bridge address, claims the anonymous 25 FLOP grant onto it, waits, then chats — collapsing the claim-to-spend window from hours to seconds so grants can't strand. Live test: address resolution works, faucet hit a transient "Indexer unavailable", chat still gates on a per-DID daily cap. Both external; the 4-hourly cron keeps retrying.
  • Backup gap closed + CLI watch added. ghost-x25519.json — the key that decrypts verification tasks — lives in a docker volume and was missing from both identity backups; now included. The collector also tracks cli.latest in releases.json: when the CLI moves past 0.1.321 (which can't speak runtime 1014020), we get emailed — it's the unblock signal for self-custodial spend and declare-gpu.
  • Testnet lesson banked. The calibration burst is an ambush — ~5 min after registration, ~3.5 min window, one shot. Devnet forgave our stale image; testnet likely won't. Register only with a fully healthy stack, verify the image against releases.json first, and treat every re-registration as a recalibration event.
